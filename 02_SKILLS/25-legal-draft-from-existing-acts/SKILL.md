# LEGAL DRAFT FROM EXISTING ACTS

## Purpose

Prepare a new legal act by combining:

- verified facts and evidence from the current case;
- applicable law;
- verified case law and doctrine;
- reusable legal modules from earlier acts;
- the best available structure and drafting style.

Do not draft from a blank page when the workspace contains a useful prior act, argument, decision, template, or procedural model.

## Mandatory workflow

Follow this order:

1. **Define the new act**
   - type of act;
   - court or authority;
   - procedural stage;
   - represented party;
   - immediate procedural objective;
   - disputed factual and legal issues.

2. **Search before drafting**
   Search the current case and related workspace materials by legal issue, not only by document title. Search for:
   - prior pleadings and appeals;
   - decisions and judgments;
   - answers to claims and submissions;
   - evidence maps and chronologies;
   - templates and forms;
   - related cases with the same legal issue;
   - cited case law and doctrine.

3. **Classify each relevant document**
   - **Model:** structure, style, headings, petitum, sequencing.
   - **Argument source:** legal reasoning, procedural objection, statutory interpretation, response to an opposing argument, case law, doctrine.
   - **Factual source:** facts, dates, parties, amounts, property, procedural events, evidence, only when it belongs to the current case.
   - **Negative model:** rejected argument, failed pleading, missing proof, procedural error, or reasoning criticized by a court.

4. **Extract legal modules**
   For every reusable module record internally:

   `LEGAL ISSUE -> POSITION -> SOURCE -> APPLICABILITY TO CURRENT CASE`

   Examples:
   - burden of proof;
   - active or passive standing;
   - limitation or forfeiture;
   - procedural violation;
   - lack of reasoning;
   - right to a reasoned decision;
   - jurisdiction;
   - costs;
   - legal construction of the petitum;
   - response to a specific opposing argument.

5. **Check transferability**
   Reuse an argument only if:
   - the legal issue and governing rule are the same or materially analogous;
   - factual differences do not defeat the analogy;
   - the legal position is not outdated;
   - the argument is not tied exclusively to facts of the old case;
   - the source is actually verified.

   Adapt the argument. Do not mechanically copy it.

6. **Build an internal drafting matrix**

   - Objective of the act;
   - current-case facts only;
   - legal issues;
   - relevant models;
   - reusable legal modules;
   - verified case law;
   - opposing arguments to neutralize;
   - procedural risks and deadlines;
   - proposed structure and petitum.

7. **Draft the act**
   Every material argument should follow, where possible:

   `CLAIM -> LEGAL BASIS -> CURRENT FACT -> EVIDENCE -> CASE LAW/POSITION -> CONCLUSION`

   The final act must read as one coherent document, not as a collage of old pleadings.

8. **Run post-draft QA**
   Check:
   - every fact belongs to the current case;
   - no names, addresses, ID numbers, case numbers, parcels, amounts, dates, courts, evidence, or procedural history leaked from another case;
   - every cited statute supports the proposition;
   - every cited decision is available and accurately described;
   - analogies are explained rather than merely cited;
   - deadlines and procedural phase are correct;
   - the petitum follows logically from the reasoning and is definite and enforceable.

## Strict case isolation

Never transfer from a model case without independent confirmation:

- names of parties;
- addresses or personal identification numbers;
- case numbers;
- dates;
- amounts;
- courts or authorities;
- parcels or property descriptions;
- evidence;
- procedural events;
- factual allegations.

Facts in the new act must come from the current case documents or an explicit user instruction. Earlier cases may supply legal knowledge, not unverified facts.

## Case law and doctrine

For every decision used, verify internally:

1. court;
2. case number;
3. date;
4. relevant legal position;
5. factual and legal analogy to the current case.

If only a citation or secondary reference is available, mark it as:

`[UNVERIFIED SOURCE — DO NOT PRESENT AS READ]`

Never invent a decision, date, quotation, holding, document, evidence, or procedural event.

## Negative-model review

Before finalizing, identify whether an earlier act or decision shows:

- an argument that failed;
- a fact that was not proved;
- a procedural mistake;
- an overbroad legal claim;
- a deficient or contradictory petitum;
- a missing document that caused rejection.

Use that material to improve the new act, not to copy the failed approach.

## Source hierarchy

Use this order of authority:

1. current-case documents and verified facts;
2. applicable legislation;
3. verified case law;
4. legal positions from decisions;
5. legal literature;
6. arguments from prior pleadings;
7. model wording and style.

A model document never overrides the law, evidence, or current-case facts.

## Zero-hallucination rule

When a fact, legal position, case-law holding, quotation, date, or document is not established from available materials, write:

`[NOT ESTABLISHED FROM AVAILABLE DOCUMENTATION]`

Do not fill gaps with assumptions. Request the missing document or formulate the point conditionally.

## Output standard

The act should be:

- self-contained;
- factually accurate;
- procedurally usable;
- source-qualified;
- adapted to the current case;
- clearly marked `NACRT — ZA REVIZIJU ADVOKATA` unless the user requests a final version.
